The NVIDIA Vera Rubin platform is the next-gen flagship compute platform NVIDIA launched after Blackwell. This article deeply analyzes it from naming origin, 6-chip package, memory subsystem, compute matrix, interconnect architecture, rack-scale solution, and software ecosystem.

## Naming Origin: In Memory of Astronomer Vera Rubin

NVIDIA chose "Vera Rubin" as the next-gen platform codename, honoring astronomer **Vera Florence Cooper Rubin** (1928-2016). Through studying galaxy rotation curves in the 1960s-70s, she **first conclusively confirmed the existence of dark matter**.

Naming the next-gen platform of the AI compute revolution after her carries deep philosophical meaning:

- **Dark matter** is the "invisible" yet dominant mass of the universe
- **AI compute** is the "invisible" yet dominant underlying infrastructure of the digital economy

## 6-Chip Package (CoWoS-L)

The Vera Rubin platform uses the industry's first **6-chip CoWoS-L package**:

| Chip | Count | Role | Process |
|------|------|------|------|
| **Vera CPU** | 1 | Host CPU / prefetch / interconnect master | TSMC 3NP |
| **Rubin GPU Die** | 2 | Matrix compute core | TSMC 3NP / 4NP |
| **I/O / HBM Base Die** | 3 | HBM4 PHY + I/O + interconnect | TSMC 4NP |

Versus the 2-chip package of Blackwell B300 Ultra (only 2 GPU dies), Vera Rubin **triples the chip count**, and transistor count grows substantially too.

## Core Specifications (per GPU)

| Item | Parameter |
|------|------|
| **Architecture** | Rubin |
| **Transistors** | ~340 billion (per GPU) |
| **Memory** | **288 GB HBM4** |
| **Memory bandwidth** | **22 TB/s** (2.75× HBM3e) |
| **FP4 Tensor (sparse)** | **50 PFLOPS** |
| **FP8 Tensor (sparse)** | 25 PFLOPS |
| **FP16/BF16 Tensor** | 12.5 PFLOPS |
| **TDP** | ~1,800 W (liquid cooling required) |

> **Data convention**: NVIDIA products after Blackwell continue to report **sparse** compute as the official figure. FP4 50 PF = 25 PF dense. For cross-vendor comparison, AMD MI400's 40 PF FP4 dense = NVIDIA Rubin R200's 80 PF FP4 sparse.

## Vera CPU Deep Dive

The Vera Rubin platform integrates NVIDIA's self-designed **ARM-compatible CPU** for the first time:

- **88 Olympus cores** (est., referencing NVIDIA Grace's 72-core Neoverse V2)
- **TSMC 3NP process**
- **1:1 paired with Rubin GPU** (1 Vera : 2 Rubin dies)
- Role: host CPU + prefetcher + NVLink 6 master + PCIe Gen 6 root complex

This is an **important milestone** in NVIDIA's CPU design — after the blocked ARM acquisition, NVIDIA chose to self-design ARM-compatible cores, similar to Apple Silicon's approach.

## NVLink 6 + ConnectX-9 Interconnect

### NVLink 6

| Item | NVLink 5 (B300) | **NVLink 6 (Rubin)** | Improvement |
|------|------------------|------------------------|------|
| Per-GPU bandwidth | 1.8 TB/s | **3.5 TB/s** | 2× |
| GPU-to-GPU hops | 1 | 1 (fully-connected switch) | Same |
| GPUs per rack | 72 | 72 | Same |

### ConnectX-9 (dual NIC)

| Item | ConnectX-8 (B300) | **ConnectX-9 (Rubin)** | Improvement |
|------|-------------------|--------------------------|------|
| Ports per GPU | 1× 1.6 Tbps | **2× 14.4 Tbps** | **18×** |
| Aggregate per GPU | 1.6 Tbps | **28.8 TB/s** | **18,000× byte rate** |
| Topology | 3D Torus | 3D Torus / fully-connected | — |

> **ConnectX-9 28.8 TB/s means the GPU is never the network bottleneck**. This is the ultimate form of "in-network computing": GPU-to-GPU communication as fast as local HBM access.

## Comparison with Concurrent Competitors

| Metric | Rubin R200 | AMD MI400 | TPU 8t | Trainium 3 |
|------|------------|-----------|--------|------------|
| Memory | 288 GB HBM4 | 432 GB HBM4 | 216 GB HBM | 144 GB HBM |
| Memory bandwidth | 22 TB/s | 19.6 TB/s | 6,528 GB/s | ~4.5 TB/s |
| FP4 compute | 50 PF (sparse) | 40 PF (dense) | — | — |
| FP8 compute | 25 PF (sparse) | 20 PF (dense) | 7 PF | 5.7 PF |
| Interconnect | NVLink 6 + CX-9 | UALink | 3D Torus | NeuronLink-v4 |
| TDP | 1,800 W | 1,000 W | N/A | 700 W |
| CPU | Vera 88-core | EPYC Venice | Arm Axion | Xeon |

> **NVIDIA advantages**: per-GPU compute (25 PF FP8 dense), NVLink interconnect, ConnectX-9 network, CUDA ecosystem.
> **AMD advantages**: single-card memory (432 GB), open UALink interconnect, Helios 72-GPU rack integration.
> **Google advantages**: JAX training paradigm, pod-scale (9,216 chips) ultra-large scale.
> **AWS advantages**: perf per dollar (4.4× vs Trainium 2), Trn3 UltraServer 144 chips.
