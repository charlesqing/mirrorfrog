The bottleneck of AI compute has shifted from **compute itself** to **memory bandwidth and capacity**. **HBM (High Bandwidth Memory)**, a core component of AI chips, will reach a **$80B+ market** in 2026 — yet there are only **3 global suppliers**: **SK Hynix, Samsung, and Micron**. This article analyzes this "memory three-kingdom war" in depth.

## Why HBM Is Critical in the AI Era

### The Memory Wall

AI model size has grown from BERT (340M parameters) in 2018 to Llama 3 (405B) in 2024 and Gemini (est. 1T+ in 2026), with **compute growing 1000×** but **memory bandwidth only growing 10-20×**.

This is the famous **von Neumann bottleneck**:

| Metric | 2018 (V100) | 2024 (H100) | 2026 (Rubin R200) | Growth |
|--------|-------------|-------------|-------------------|--------|
| Compute (FP16/BF16) | 125 TFLOPS | 989 TFLOPS | 25 PFLOPS | 200× |
| Memory capacity | 32 GB | 80 GB | 288 GB | 9× |
| Memory bandwidth | 900 GB/s | 3.35 TB/s | 22 TB/s | 24× |

> **Compute grows far faster than memory bandwidth**, leaving GPUs frequently "waiting for data." HBM is the core to relieving this bottleneck.

### HBM vs GDDR vs SRAM

| Memory type | Bandwidth (per pin) | Capacity density | Power | Use case |
|-------------|---------------------|-----------------|-------|----------|
| **HBM4** | **6.4 Gbps/pin** | High (12-Hi) | Medium | AI training / inference GPU |
| HBM3e | 4.8 Gbps/pin | High (8-Hi/12-Hi) | Medium | AI training / inference GPU |
| HBM3 | 4.0 Gbps/pin | Medium | Medium | AI training |
| HBM2e | 3.2 Gbps/pin | Medium | Medium | AI inference |
| GDDR6X | 1.6 Gbps/pin | Medium | Medium-high | Consumer GPU |
| GDDR7 | 2.5 Gbps/pin | Medium | Medium | Consumer / workstation |
| LPDDR5X | 0.85 Gbps/pin | High | Low | Edge AI / mobile |
| SRAM (on-chip) | 10+ Gbps/pin | Extremely low | Extremely high | LPU / cache |

> HBM is the best balance of bandwidth and capacity. **SRAM is fastest but too small (100× the cost per GB of HBM)**, while **GDDR has large capacity but insufficient bandwidth**.

## The Three-Vendor Landscape

### 1. SK Hynix: Current HBM Hegemon (70% share)

**SK Hynix** is the **absolute leader** of the HBM market:

| Item | SK Hynix status |
|------|-----------------|
| **Market share** | **~70%** (2025 total HBM market) |
| **HBM4 progress** | **First mass production Q1 2026**, exclusive NVIDIA supply |
| **HBM3e supply** | Primary NVIDIA supplier (80%), partial AMD |
| **Core technology** | **Advanced MR-MUF** (Mass Reflow Molded Underfill) |
| **Capacity** | 2026 plan: **25,000 HBM wafers/month** |
| **Key customers** | NVIDIA (90%), AMD, partial Google |
| **2025 HBM revenue** | ~$30B (+80% YoY) |
| **2025 net margin** | ~35% (far above traditional DRAM business) |

#### SK Hynix Key Advantages

- **Earliest HBM3 mass production (2018)**: first-mover technology
- **Deep NVIDIA partnership**: HBM3 and HBM3e both debuted with NVIDIA
- **Advanced MR-MUF process**: leading packaging yield
- **HBM4 first-mover**: first mass production Q1 2026

#### Key Events

| Time | Event |
|------|-------|
| 2014 | Co-developed HBM with AMD |
| 2018 | First HBM2 mass production (NVIDIA V100) |
| 2020 | HBM2e mass production |
| 2022 | HBM3 mass production (NVIDIA H100) |
| 2024 | HBM3e 12-Hi mass production (NVIDIA B200) |
| Q1 2026 | **HBM4 first mass production** (NVIDIA Rubin R200) |

### 2. Samsung: HBM4 Challenger (20% share)

**Samsung** is the **challenger** in the HBM market, hampered by yield and NVIDIA certification:

| Item | Samsung status |
|------|------------------|
| **Market share** | **~20%** (2025 total HBM market) |
| **HBM4 progress** | Q2 2026 mass production (one quarter behind SK Hynix) |
| **HBM3e supply** | Awaiting NVIDIA certification, mainly AMD / Google |
| **Core technology** | **TC-NCF** (Thermal Compression Non-Conductive Film) |
| **Capacity** | 2026 plan: 10,000 HBM wafers/month |
| **Key customers** | AMD (partial MI300X), Google TPU, Cerebras |
| **2025 HBM revenue** | ~$8B (+150% YoY, but only 1/4 of SK Hynix) |
| **2025 net margin** | ~5% (low margin due to low yield) |

#### Samsung Key Problems

- **HBM3 NVIDIA certification failed**: multiple sample rejections 2023-2024
- **Low yield**: HBM3e yield ~50% (SK Hynix ~70%)
- **Divergent tech路线**: Samsung bet on TC-NCF (vs SK Hynix's MR-MUF)
- **2024 heavy losses**: huge HBM investment with slow returns

#### Samsung 2025-2026 Breakthroughs

| Time | Event |
|------|-------|
| 2024-12 | HBM3e 8-Hi passes AMD certification |
| 2025-03 | HBM3e 12-Hi passes Google TPU certification |
| 2025-Q4 | HBM4 trial production, mass production Q2 2026 |
| 2025-Q4 | NVIDIA HBM3e 12-Hi certification **passed** (partial orders) |

> **At the end of 2025 Samsung finally secured partial NVIDIA HBM3e 12-Hi orders** — a turning point for Samsung's HBM business.

### 3. Micron: HBM Dark Horse (10% share)

**Micron** is the **dark horse** of the HBM market, becoming NVIDIA's second supplier with its **HBM3E 12-Hi 9.2 Gbps speed**:

| Item | Micron status |

| **Core technology** | **1znm DRAM** + Advanced Packaging |
| **Capacity** | 2026 plan: 5,000 HBM wafers/month |
| **Key customers** | NVIDIA (partial), AMD, Intel |
| **2025 HBM revenue** | ~$4B (+200% YoY) |
| **2025 net margin** | ~10% |

#### Micron Key Advantages

- **HBM3E 12-Hi 9.2 Gbps**: industry's highest speed (ahead of SK Hynix's 9 Gbps)
- **US domestic manufacturing**: Idaho/New York fabs, CHIPS Act compliant
- **NVIDIA second supplier**: avoids single-source risk
- **2025 breakthrough**: +200% YoY revenue, fastest growth of the three

#### Micron Key Events

| Time | Event |

> **Micron is the fastest-growing of the three, +200% YoY in 2025.** But capacity is only 5,000 wafers/month, limiting its share growth.

## Risks and Challenges

### 1. Tight HBM Capacity

- **2026 shortfall of 20%** (demand 50K wafers vs supply 40K)
- **NVIDIA Rubin R200 shipments** may be delayed by tight HBM supply
- **Customer advance deposits** have become the norm

### 2. US Export Controls

- **HBM exports to China** are strictly limited by the US Commerce Department
- **Samsung, SK Hynix** factories in China are restricted
- **Huawei** accelerates domestic CXMT HBM substitution

### 3. Divergent Technology Routes

- **SK Hynix**: MR-MUF route, leading
- **Samsung**: TC-NCF route, behind but catching up
- **Micron**: somewhere in between

### 4. Competing Technologies

- **Samsung HBM-PIM**: processing unit inside HBM (in-memory compute)
- **TSMC SoIC**: 3D-stacked SRAM + Logic
- **Micron HBM-CX**: Compute Express Link integration

## Future Outlook

### Short term (2026-2027)

- **HBM remains tight**: demand > supply
- **High prices**: HBM4 $20-25/GB
- **Three vendors coexist**: SK Hynix 70% + Samsung 20% + Micron 10%

### Medium term (2027-2029)

- **HBM4E / HBM5**: capacity 64-96 GB, speed 20-28 Gbps
- **Domestic HBM rise**: CXMT mass-produces 8-Hi
- **New entrants**: YMTC (Yangtze Memory) may enter the HBM market

### Long term (2029+)

- **HBM6 / 3D HBM**: more layers stacked, TSV alternatives
- **PIM-HBM**: processing unit inside HBM
- **Alternative technologies**: on-chip SRAM capacity breakthroughs (e.g., Cerebras WSE)

## Conclusion

The HBM three-way war will last 3-5 years:

1. **SK Hynix is unshakable in the short term** — HBM4 debut + deep NVIDIA binding
2. **Samsung broke through at end of 2025** — HBM3e 12-Hi finally certified by NVIDIA
3. **Micron is the fastest grower** — HBM3E 9.2 Gbps, industry's fastest
4. **Capacity remains tight** — 2026 shortfall of 20%
5. **Domestic HBM rises** — CXMT ChangXin 4 Tbps breakthrough

**HBM is not a supporting act — it is the "water, electricity, and coal" of the AI era.**
