# NVIDIA H200 (Hopper Refresh)

## Product Overview

The NVIDIA H200, released in 2024, is a memory-upgraded version of the H100. The key change is the upgrade from 80GB HBM3 to **141GB HBM3e**, with bandwidth increasing from 3.35 TB/s to **4.8 TB/s** (+43%). This delivers significant performance gains for 70B+ parameter model inference, enabling single-GPU loading of longer contexts.

## November 2026 Update: Import Approvals and Secondary-Market Pricing

Reuters reported on November 5 that roughly 600,000 H200 accelerators are sitting in warehouses in Hong Kong, China, because mainland authorities have not approved large-scale imports. U.S. rules permit exports to approved Chinese customers, but only narrow licences have been issued — Alibaba, ByteDance and Tencent each received about 10,000 units for training use only. The same reporting puts eight-GPU H200 servers above 6 million yuan on the secondary market, roughly three times the price a year earlier. Follow-up reporting on November 7 says about 24 Chinese companies have been cleared to buy or handle H200 chips, with some registered offices apparently vacant and ownership links to restricted entities, raising questions about licence due diligence.

All of the above is press reporting, not a first-party regulatory or NVIDIA document; the stockpile size, prices and company list are unconfirmed and are not entered into the specification table. ## 2026-11-19 update: $2.4B from China in the quarter, zero in guidance

The FY2027 Q3 results published on November 18 disclose $2.4B in revenue from licensed Hopper data center products sold into China. Q4 guidance is $130B plus or minus 3% and explicitly assumes no data center compute revenue from China. Read together, the message is clear. This is not a demand call; the company is leaving no room for that revenue in guidance.

The call also added a pricing datapoint. Hopper GPUs have recontracted under multi-year agreements at up to twice their original price, while token prices have dropped nearly 60% since May and rental rates across GPU generations have been rising. For customers still running H-series clusters, renewal cost and new purchase cost are no longer on the same curve.

> Sourcing note: the $2.4B figure is company-disclosed revenue for licensed Hopper products in China, with no model split. "Twice the original price" is the upper end of a recontracting range described on the call, not a list price. Nothing here changes the spec table.
