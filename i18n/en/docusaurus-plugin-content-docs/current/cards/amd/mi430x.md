# AMD Instinct MI430X (CDNA 5, HPC / Sovereign AI)

## Product Overview

**AMD Instinct MI430X** is the **MI400 series HPC / sovereign AI SKU** announced at **Advancing AI 2026 (July)**, targeting high-precision scientific computing and national-scale compute projects. It shares the CDNA 5 architecture and the **432GB HBM4 / 23.3TB/s** memory configuration with the MI455X; the biggest difference is **native hardware-level FP64**: **288 TFLOPS** (**8.7x** that of the NVIDIA Vera Rubin).

The MI430X has been locked in for two national supercomputing systems:
- 🇺🇸 **US ORNL "Discovery"** (Oak Ridge National Laboratory, deployment in 2028)
- 🇪🇺 **France's "Alice Recoque"** (built jointly by GENCI + CEA)

AMD officially confirmed on the August 4 earnings call that the MI430X is already running in the **ORNL and Jean Zay (France)** exascale supercomputers. **Shipping 2027 H1**.

## 2026-11-13 update: AMD restates FP64 and bandwidth

An editor's note in AMD's November 12 MI400 series post added detail on MI430X: native FP64, 432 GB of HBM4, up to 23.3 TB/s of memory bandwidth, positioned for HPC and scientific computing. AMD also published an analysis across 47 workload configurations in 11 applications to show where native double precision is required rather than emulated.

The post did not revise the FP64 figure or the shipping date. The 288 TFLOPS hardware FP64 and first-half-2027 availability on this page stay as previously recorded. The same announcement covered the MI450X and MI455X inference result; see the [MI450 series page](/docs/cards/amd/mi450).

> Sourcing note: vendor editor's note, not a new specification sheet. The 47-configuration analysis is AMD's own scope and has not been independently reviewed.

## 2026-11-17 update: MI430X moves to the center of AMD's HPC story at SC26

At SC26 (November 15-20, Chicago), AMD showed MI430X alongside 6th Gen EPYC (Venice). The message shifted from the previous day's spec clarification to a full system story: EuroHPC has selected MI430X and 6th Gen EPYC for LUMI-AI, expected online in the second half of 2027. Oak Ridge's Lux system on the same floor uses MI355X and ranks 14th on TOP500 with 220.79 PFLOPS HPL. That is context, not an MI430X result.

The 288 TFLOPS native FP64 figure remains an AMD target, not a measured result. The previous section already recorded 432 GB of HBM4 and up to 23.3 TB/s; there is no need to repeat them. AMD also says its technology appears in 202 TOP500 systems, four of the top ten. AMD counts any system using any AMD technology, while TOP500 lists 199 systems by CPU brand. The two counts use different rules.

All three vendors talked double precision that week. NVIDIA gave Rubin HPC a core count but no FP64 number; AMD gave an FP64 target for a machine due in 2027; Intel brought a pre-production D270 against a 600W RTX PRO 6000. FP64 is back as a selling point, but the evidence behind each claim varies too much to rank them yet.

> Sourcing note: SC26 displays, the EuroHPC selection and AMD's stated targets. The 288 TFLOPS figure is a planned target and may change before release; the 202-system count is AMD's own method. The spec table is unchanged.

## 2026-11-19 update: AMD quantifies where FP64 emulation breaks

At SC26 AMD published a number more useful than another spec sheet. Across 47 workload configurations in 11 applications, only one application was a partial fit for Ozaki schemes that rebuild FP64 from low-precision tensor-core math, or under 10% of the set. The paper is titled "FP64 Performance and the Limits of Ozaki Emulation on HPC Systems." AMD says these schemes only pay off on large matrices, are sensitive to input scaling, and can use roughly three times the memory of a native FP64 multiply. HBM per GPU is fixed, so a bigger footprint means more GPUs, racks and power.

The paper aims squarely at the strategy of giving die area to low-precision AI math and filling the double-precision gap with emulation. AMD's conclusion is that sparse iterative linear algebra, stencils and hydrodynamics, high-order finite elements, Monte Carlo and particle transport, and molecular dynamics cannot easily be reshaped into one dense matrix multiply.

The customer list is now specific: Discovery at Oak Ridge (the Frontier successor), Alice Recoque in France, Herder at HLRS in Germany and LUMI-AI at CSC in Finland. One caveat: AMD says the 288 TFLOPS figure comes from analytical models and preliminary silicon targets, still not measurements. The previous section called it a planned target; this adds the source. The number in the spec table is unchanged.

> Sourcing note: AMD's SC26 paper and vendor commentary. AMD selected the 47 configurations and 11 applications; no third party has reproduced the study. The threefold memory figure is AMD's estimate and varies by implementation and workload.
